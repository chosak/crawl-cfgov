CFPB Joins Department of Justice and Other Federal Partners in Anti-Scam Efforts

NOV 24, 2026
Share & print

Today, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) joined the Department of Justice (DOJ) and federal partners to announce new and ongoing anti-scam efforts and protect consumers from financial fraud.

Since March 2026, the CFPB has worked with DOJ and participating agencies, including the U.S. Department of Homeland Security, U.S. Department of State, U.S. Department of the Treasury, Federal Bureau of Investigation, Federal Communications Commission, Federal Trade Commission, Internal Revenue Service, and the U.S. Postal Inspection Service, as part of a coordinated anti-scam initiative. The CFPB has contributed consumer education, outreach, research, and supervisory expertise to broader federal efforts to identify emerging threats, prevent fraud, and protect consumers.

CFPB actions to prevent fraud and protect consumers

As part of its ongoing work with federal partners, the CFPB has advanced several fraud-prevention activities since March 2026, including:

Protection for servicemembers, veterans, military families, older adults, and caregivers

  • Provided continuing training to military legal professionals and military personnel on financial protection laws, frauds and scams, and data impacting veterans, members of the military and their families.
  • Served as an active member of the Veteran Scam and Fraud Evasion (VSAFE) Task Force, which is a multi-agency federal initiative created to protect veterans, military personnel, and their families from financial scams, identity theft, and predatory fraud. Coordinated by the Department of Veterans Affairs, it includes representatives from the Department of Education, Federal Trade Commission, Social Security Administration, Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, Department of State, and Department of Defense.
  • The Office of Older Americans, including through its Elder Fraud Prevention and Response Network (EEPRN), has participated in a series of educational webinars on preventing fraud, including combatting government imposters scams, educating caregivers on preventing elder financial fraud, and sharing elder fraud prevention resources with local law enforcement, federal agencies and industry organizations.
  • Partnered with the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) in developing programming as part of the joint Money Smart for Older Americans Program , which raises awareness among older adults and their caregivers on how to prevent fraud, scams, and other elder financial exploitation, that focuses on the prevention of cyber scams.

Increased consumer education and community outreach

  • Published and maintained online frauds and scams resources, including dedicated tools that help the public recognize, prevent and report scams. These online resources discuss common types of scams, how to spot identity theft, and how consumers can protect themselves from fraud and scams and have so far this year have been viewed more than a quarter of a million times.
  • As part of its work to equip consumers with tools and resources to identify and avoid increasingly sophisticated and ever-evolving fraud and scams, published the Financial Literacy Annual Report which includes information dedicated to discussing frauds and scams, including fraud and scams among young adults, older adults, and servicemembers.
  • As a member of the Financial Literacy and Education Commission (FLEC), the CFPB supports the new National Strategy for Financial Literacy , which includes a priority area focused on scams and fraud education. The strategy aims to equip consumers with the knowledge and tools to recognize, avoid, and mitigate the impact of fraud and scams. To support this goal, the CFPB provides evidence-based financial education resources and research-driven initiatives that help consumers build financial knowledge, develop sound financial habits, and make informed financial decisions. The National Strategy was developed through collaboration among FLEC agencies, public input, and independent research on financial literacy and education.

Research, supervision, complaints, and interagency coordination

  • Accepted and reported on consumers’ complaints related to companies offering consumer financial products and services, including reports of fraudulent or fraudulent induced transactions. The CFPB catalogs these activities as part of the statutorily required Consumer Response Annual Report.
  • Directed consumers who called the CFPB’s toll-free number to educational resources and places they could report scams (i.e., Federal Trade Commission, Federal Bureau of Investigation, local law enforcement departments, local adult protective services, and state attorneys general).
  • Conducted technology examinations that focus on information technology controls that support compliance with federal consumer financial laws. These controls ensure the financial systems have protections that prevent harm to consumers, such as the loss of information and data that could be used in cybercrimes, fraud, or predatory schemes.

New CFPB resources to help consumers spot scams

Building on these efforts, the CFPB is participating in a coordinated public awareness campaign to help consumers recognize and avoid scams during the holiday season.

The CFPB has also launched a new webpage to help consumers identify and report scams involving individuals impersonating CFPB employees. The resource provides guidance on verifying suspicious communications, recognizing warning signs, and reporting suspected scams.

Consumers should be aware that the CFPB will never contact consumers to demand payment or request sensitive financial information.

Anyone receiving suspicious communication claiming to be from the CFPB can verify its legitimacy by calling (855) 411-2372 and report suspected scams to the Federal Trade Commission at ReportFraud.ftc.gov .

Find more information about frauds and scams at consumerfinance.gov/fraud.
